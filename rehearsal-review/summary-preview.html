WEBINAR SUMMARY · FINANCE & PAYROLL

Year-end payroll in the Philippines: fix the data in October so December runs clean

CONDUCTED ONFriday, 9 October 2026
LANGUAGEEnglish

Sharon Aytona of Bridge PayDay Solutions and Thinktank Professional Services walked Philippine HR, payroll and finance teams through the year-end rules they can't miss: 13th-month pay, the ₱90,000 tax-exempt ceiling, annualization and the January BIR deadlines. Manolo Garcia-Ramos of PeoplesHR then showed how one system and anomaly flags catch payroll errors before the run. The session closed with live Q&A.

WATCH THE RECORDING AND LEARN HOW TO

1. Compute 13th-month pay and know what to leave out

2. Plan a September-to-December timeline that avoids the December rush

3. Answer the audit questions finance and employees will ask

Key takeaways

Watch from 17:40

Count the 13th month inside the ₱90,000 ceiling. It already includes 13th-month pay and other benefits, so bonuses above it are taxable even if the offer says tax-exempt.

Watch from 20:26

Use basic salary only. Total basic salary earned January to December, less absences, divided by 12. Allowances, overtime and premium pay stay out.

Watch from 27:41

Start in September or October. Validate master data first, reconcile and test-run annualization in November, and keep December for final checks, reporting and payout.

Watch from 30:03

Annualize every month. Computing tax monthly as if for the full year avoids underpayment and heavy December deductions, so take-home pay stays steady.

Watch from 32:29

Build a preparer, reviewer and approver chain. Be ready to show where a number came from, what changed, who approved it and how to reproduce it.

Watch from 42:42

Catch problems before payroll. Late overtime approvals, unreported promotions and leave filed after cutoff all start upstream. Payroll is only where they land.
