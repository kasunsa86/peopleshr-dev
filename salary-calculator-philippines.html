How take-home pay is calculated in the Philippines

Net pay is gross pay minus the employee's SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG contributions, BIR withholding tax and any other deductions. The contributions are deducted before tax, so withholding tax is worked out on what's left. On top of gross pay, the employer pays its own SSS (plus EC), PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG shares.

Frequently asked questions

How is withholding tax calculated?

Under the TRAIN law withholding table in force from 1 January 2023, monthly taxable compensation up to ₱20,833 is tax-free. Above that, rates step up through 15%, 20%, 25% and 30% to 35% on compensation over ₱666,667. Taxable compensation is gross taxable pay minus the employee's SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG contributions. See the Bureau of Internal Revenue.

How are SSS contributions worked out?

From January 2025 the SSS contribution is 15% of the Monthly Salary Credit (MSC): 5% from the employee and 10% from the employer. MSC runs from ₱5,000 to ₱35,000 in ₱500 steps, and the part above ₱20,000 goes to the Mandatory Provident Fund. The employer also pays Employees' Compensation (EC) of ₱10, or ₱30 when MSC is ₱15,000 or more. See the Social Security System.

What about PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG?

The PhilHealth premium is 5% of monthly basic salary, with a ₱10,000 floor and ₱100,000 ceiling, split equally between employee and employer. Allowances, overtime and bonuses are not included (PhilHealth Advisory 2025-0002). Pag-IBIG is 2% from each side on up to ₱10,000 of monthly fund salary, which is basic salary plus allowances, so the standard employee share tops out at ₱200. Employees earning ₱1,500 or less pay 1% (Pag-IBIG Fund).

How are minimum wage earners treated?

Minimum wage earners are exempt from income tax on their statutory minimum wage, holiday pay, overtime, night shift differential and hazard pay. Other taxable compensation they receive is still taxed. Tick "Minimum wage earner" and mark any taxable allowances.

How does Net to Gross work?

Enter the take-home pay you want to pay. The calculator keeps your allowances and deductions as entered and finds the basic salary that produces that net amount after contributions, withholding tax and deductions. Just past an SSS or Pag-IBIG step, two salaries can give the same take-home pay; the calculator shows the lower one.

Is 13th-month pay included?

No. 13th-month pay and other benefits are exempt up to ₱90,000 a year combined, and anything above that is taxed in the year-end annualization. For annualization and an illustrative BIR Form 2316, use our Philippines Payroll & Tax Calculator.

Philippines payroll reference

Rates & Rules

BIR withholding tax, January 2023 onwards

  • Up to ₱20,833: 0%
  • ₱20,833 – 33,333: 15% of the excess over ₱20,833
  • ₱33,333 – 66,667: ₱1,875 + 20% of the excess over ₱33,333
  • ₱66,667 – 166,667: ₱8,541.80 + 25% of the excess over ₱66,667
  • ₱166,667 – 666,667: ₱33,541.80 + 30% of the excess over ₱166,667
  • Above ₱666,667: ₱183,541.80 + 35% of the excess over ₱666,667
  • Employee SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG contributions are deducted before tax.
  • De minimis benefits are exempt only up to their ceilings (RR 29-2025). 13th-month pay and other benefits are exempt up to ₱90,000 a year.

Source: Bureau of Internal Revenue (RA 10963 TRAIN, RR 11-2018)

SSS, effective January 2025

  • 15% of Monthly Salary Credit: 5% employee, 10% employer.
  • MSC from ₱5,000 to ₱35,000 in ₱500 steps; MSC above ₱20,000 goes to the MPF.
  • Employer-only EC: ₱10 below ₱15,000 MSC, ₱30 from ₱15,000 MSC.
  • Based on compensation, which includes allowances.

Source: SSS Circular 2024-006

PhilHealth

  • 5% premium on monthly basic salary, shared 2.5% employee and 2.5% employer.
  • ₱10,000 floor and ₱100,000 ceiling.
  • Basic salary only: allowances, overtime, 13th-month pay and bonuses are excluded.

Source: PhilHealth Advisory 2025-0002

Pag-IBIG / HDMF, effective February 2024

  • Employee: 1% at ₱1,500 or below, otherwise 2%.
  • Employer: 2%.
  • Fund salary is basic salary plus allowances, up to ₱10,000, so the standard employee share is at most ₱200.
  • Voluntary savings above this are an after-tax deduction.

Source: Pag-IBIG Fund

Important: This is an informational estimator. Actual payroll can depend on year-to-date records, previous employers, 13th-month pay and bonuses, regional minimum wages and the latest BIR, SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG issuances.
