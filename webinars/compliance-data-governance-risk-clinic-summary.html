WEBINAR SUMMARY · FINANCE & PAYROLL

Payroll panic starts at the attendance device: a risk clinic for attendance data and PDPA compliance

FORMATOn-demand webinar
CONDUCTED ONWednesday, 7 October 2026
LANGUAGEEnglish

Two clinics in one session. Kapila Gajapala traces payroll errors back to attendance devices, approvals and access, and shows how to catch them early. Mahinda Pathirana, Chief Information Security Officer, explains what Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act asks of HR, and how to be ready for an audit tomorrow.

WATCH THE RECORDING AND LEARN HOW TO

1. Fix attendance problems before payroll cut-off

2. Keep devices, access and exits in sync with HR

3. Get HR data ready for a PDPA audit

Key takeaways

Watch from 08:22

Payroll errors start with attendance. Incomplete records, late overtime and leave approvals, and missing punches all end up on the payslip. Approve and fix yesterday’s records today instead of in a rush before cut-off.

Watch from 14:29

Plan the month on a calendar. Set clear cut-off dates and times for leave, attendance checks and approvals, so the whole month leads calmly to payroll day.

Watch from 18:33

Give employees back the transparency. Punch cards once showed every worker their own hours. Kiosks and the mobile app let employees check attendance, overtime and leave before payroll runs, so errors are caught early.

Watch from 20:35

Monitor devices before the morning rush. A dashboard shows which devices are online, and email or SMS alerts flag problems early. A responsibility matrix makes it clear who fixes power, network or device faults.

Watch from 27:43

Sync device access with HR. Link devices to the HR system so transfers move fingerprints to the new branch, and leavers are removed on their exit date, not left with access to the premises.

Watch from 42:01

Classify your data before the auditor asks. Sri Lanka's PDPA expects HR to classify and label data, control who can access it, train staff, use MFA and single sign-on, encrypt data and log changes to confidential records.

Employee records are kept for seven years to meet legal requirements, then moved to cold storage. Once an employee is made inactive, only a designated administrator can view their data, and it is removed from attendance devices.
Use internal or licensed storage with encryption and data labeling, covered by an agreement with the provider, and encrypt communication channels with HTTPS and TLS.
