WEBINAR SUMMARY · HR & PEOPLE

Buying AI is the easy part: lessons from transforming HR at scale, from head office to the mine site

FORMATOn-demand webinar
CONDUCTED ONThursday, 3 September 2026
LANGUAGEBahasa Indonesia

Gustia R. Anasril, Chief HRGS Officer at Muliabara Group, has led HR through mergers, an IPO and a nickel operation that grew its workforce 35-fold in two years. He explains why AI transformation starts with clean data and redesigned work, not software, and how it is changing succession and skills in mining. The session was held in Bahasa Indonesia.

WATCH THE RECORDING AND LEARN HOW TO

1. Fix HR data and processes before adding AI

2. Use AI responsibly in people decisions

3. Plan succession and skills with live data

Key takeaways

Watch from 09:19

Buying the tool is not the transformation. Most organizations use 20% or less of what their AI can do. The hard part is changing roles, decision rights, skills, habits and KPIs. A toll road only gets you lost faster if the exits are a mess.

Watch from 17:30

Fix the data first. To AI, "Supervisor", "SPV" and "Acting supervisor" are different jobs. Simplify processes, standardize data, digitalize workflows, then add intelligence. AI makes a broken process break faster.

Watch from 20:35

Speak in productivity, cost and risk. Directors respond to numbers. Every HR AI project should show its effect on productivity, cost or risk, such as output per head, overtime, turnover or succession risk.

Watch from 29:49

Four non-negotiables for responsible AI. Explainability, fairness, human accountability and data protection. AI is the car navigator: it can suggest a route, but a person decides whether to promote someone.

Watch from 34:57

Succession becomes continuous. Instead of a once-a-year 9-box meeting, AI flags single-successor roles, rising talent and new skills as they happen. It only works with a clear skills taxonomy, and human judgment still decides.

Watch from 45:10

A five-step playbook. Choose the work, fix the foundation, measure one use case, build in trust from the start and scale behavior, not just licenses. Ask what work should be redesigned because of AI, not just what AI can automate.

Q&A highlights

Start with validated candidate data, then define a clear taxonomy of what you want, such as target universities, minimum GPA or years of experience in the same role. AI only does what you ask it to, and it can be wrong, so set the criteria carefully.
In HR, it can help with recruitment, talent mapping and promotion decisions, even summarizing recorded video interviews. Beyond HR, AI can handle customer service chatbots and automate repetitive processes. Map your business processes first, then decide where to start.
