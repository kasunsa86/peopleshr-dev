WEBINAR SUMMARY · FINANCE & PAYROLL

Year-end payroll breaks before December: a readiness check for Philippine HR and Finance teams

CONDUCTED ONFriday, 9 October 2026
LANGUAGEEnglish

Sharon Aytona of Bridge PayDay Solutions and Thinktank Professional Services walked Philippine HR and Finance teams through the year-end crunch: the 13th-month rules, the ₱90,000 ceiling, the mistakes that surface in December and the BIR deadlines that follow in January. Manolo Garcia-Ramos then showed how PeoplesHR flags payroll anomalies before you run payroll.

WATCH THE RECORDING AND LEARN HOW TO

1. Compute 13th-month pay without the usual mistakes

2. Plan year-end from October, not December

3. Catch payroll anomalies before you run payroll

Key takeaways

Watch from 15:39

Pay 13th-month on or before December 24. Any payout frequency works. Set an internal policy and keep to it, even when the people in HR change.

Watch from 17:22

Mind the ₱90,000 ceiling. It covers 13th-month pay and other benefits together. Anything above it is taxable, even if the offer letter calls it non-taxable.

Watch from 20:32

The formula is simple, the data isn't. Add the basic salary earned from January to December, less absences, and divide by 12. Allowances, overtime and premium pay are excluded.

Watch from 27:49

Start in October, not December. Validate master data in October, reconcile and test annualization in November, and leave December for final checks, reporting and payouts.

Watch from 29:53

Annualize every month. Computing tax each month as if for the whole year avoids a large catch-up deduction in December and keeps take-home pay steady.

Watch from 44:38

Catch anomalies before you run payroll. PeoplesHR flags unusual pay, overtime and allowance changes against thresholds you set, then shows the whole run on one dashboard before approval.

Q&A highlights

13th-month pay is based on the basic salary actually earned during the calendar year. Unpaid absences, lates and undertime that reduce the basic salary are therefore reflected in the computation.
It is the annual schedule. It changes only if the BIR issues an advisory or an extension, which in the speakers' experience tends to come at the very last minute.
Correct them, ideally every month rather than in December. Errors like these lead to over- or underpaid tax, and employees need steady pay for their budgets, not a large deduction at year-end.
Sharon recommended outsourcing annualization, especially with hundreds of employees and their 2316 forms to prepare. It also gives the year's work an external check. Choose a partner who can advise on your monthly payroll too.
Employee data, classifications and movements are captured once and flow straight into payroll, with no downloading and uploading. In PeoplesHR, processing the December 31 payroll completes the annualization and prepares the 2316 forms and the alphalist automatically.
Something that looks unusual compared with the expected pattern from past payroll data, such as a sudden spike. It doesn't automatically mean it's wrong. It signals something worth checking before you run payroll.
Ask for ISO 27001 certification, SOC Type 1 or Type 2 reports, and Philippine data privacy certification.
Set a service window, for example three days from payslip release to raise concerns, and say so when the payslips go out. An HRIS chatbot can also answer common employee questions instantly, around the clock.
