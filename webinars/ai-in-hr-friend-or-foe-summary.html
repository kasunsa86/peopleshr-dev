WEBINAR SUMMARY · HR & PEOPLE

Friend or foe? AI in HR leans friend, if you measure it and keep people in charge

FORMATOn-demand webinar
CONDUCTED ONTuesday, 18 June 2024
LANGUAGEEnglish

Co-hosted with Global Frontiers and EBS Ghana. Luxsho Logan of PeoplesHR shows how AI already helps with job descriptions, screening, onboarding and reskilling, and where bias, privacy and black-box decisions call for caution. Dr. Kwame Annor adds why HR in Ghana must close the AI skills gap.

WATCH THE RECORDING AND LEARN HOW TO

1. Use AI in recruitment, onboarding and reskilling

2. Spot bias, privacy and black-box risks early

3. Start with one function and clear KPIs

Key takeaways

Watch from 05:12

Leaders expect to fall behind without AI. Gartner found 76% of HR leaders believe they will lag peers if they do not adopt AI within 12 to 24 months, and business leaders feel the same pressure.

Watch from 07:16

Recruitment is the quickest win. Generative AI drafts current, bias-aware job descriptions in seconds and writes personalized outreach to passive candidates who are not looking at job boards.

Watch from 13:23

Poor onboarding drives early turnover. Much turnover happens in the first six months, and replacing someone can cost up to 300% of their salary. Automated welcome emails, introductions and calendar invites can cut that sharply.

Watch from 15:25

Reskill before you rehire. AI can suggest the right learning for each person based on role, feedback and projects, helping move people internally, for example from marketing into sales.

Watch from 22:33

Know the limits. Public AI tools don't know your organization, models inherit their trainers' biases, local privacy laws may restrict what you analyze, and unexplained black-box decisions are risky when they affect people.

Watch from 35:49

Check AI against local law. Dr. Kwame asked an AI tool for Ghana's labor practices. It returned eight where the law has three, mixing in other countries' labor acts. HR needs training to spot answers like that.
