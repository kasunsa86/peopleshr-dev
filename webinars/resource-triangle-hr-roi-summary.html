WEBINAR SUMMARY · HR & PEOPLE

The right software, the wrong room: why technology, finance and people must decide together

CONDUCTED ONFriday, 5 June 2026
LANGUAGEBahasa Indonesia

Marcino Waas, HR Technology Strategist at Pratesis, explains why so many software projects stall after the contract is signed: the technology is right and the budget approved, but the people are not ready. He shares the resource triangle framework for getting technology, finance and HR to decide together. The session was held in Bahasa Indonesia.

WATCH THE RECORDING AND LEARN HOW TO

1. Spot why software adoption stalls after go-live

2. Bring technology, finance and HR into one decision

3. Assess digital maturity before you buy

Key takeaways

Watch from 07:14

Good software can still fail. A state-owned company bought a world-class project management system for its strategic projects. Staff only updated it when reports were due, and soon went back to Excel. The technology and budget were right; the people were not ready.

Watch from 11:20

Adoption stalls, then tech takes the blame. Four months after go-live, adoption is stuck at 40%, people cannot find the approve button and old habits return. A year on, the system becomes the scapegoat for a people problem.

Watch from 14:24

Three engines that must run together. Technology asks whether the software fits and how it will evolve. Finance works out the total cost of ownership over three to five years. People decide whether the organization can actually use it.

Watch from 18:28

Look for transferable skills first. Marcino's example: an Indonesian electronics maker moving into electric vehicles hired petrol-car engineers who could not do the work. It found the right skills in its own washing-machine technicians, who already worked with electric motors.

Watch from 23:37

Sitting together avoids buying twice. Finance may plan to buy a claims tool the HR system already has, or IT may own an automation tool HR could use. A joint meeting catches the duplicates.

Watch from 29:46

Give HR a seat at the strategy table. Whatever technology or budget you choose, people run it. Bring HR into purchase and planning decisions, not just to recruit and train afterward.

Q&A highlights

SOPs and policies have to be remapped and behavior changes. Time freed up by efficiency has to be filled with meaningful work, for example when chatbots reduce help-desk calls. Technology also moves faster than implementation, and some people hold on to old skills and blame the system.
SaaS is usually quick. An organization under 100 people with clean data can go live in two to four weeks, and most small implementations take about two months. A large, heavily customized or on-premise roll-out can take a year. Collecting clean data usually takes longest.
Start with engagement. Dashboards showing attendance, leave, lateness and length of service help HR notice patterns, such as someone stuck in the same role for five years or frequent absences linked to medical claims, and reach out with care before they leave.
