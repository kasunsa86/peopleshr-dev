Best HRIS Software in the Philippines: 2026 Buyer's Guide

Updated October 2026 · 14 min read

Choosing the right HRIS software in the Philippines is a crucial decision for any organization. With strict labor rules, various government agencies, and ongoing payroll and attendance requirements, businesses need a system that goes beyond just storing basic employee data. They need a platform that ensures compliance, boosts accuracy, and streamlines daily HR tasks.

The best HRIS for your organization is the system that can support your actual workforce, payroll rules and approval processes, and demonstrate that fit before the contract is signed. For an organization with hundreds of employees, several locations or rotating shifts, a feature checklist is only the starting point. The stronger question is: Can this system handle our exceptions without sending HR back to spreadsheets?

This guide outlines what to prioritize when evaluating HRIS platforms in the Philippines, which HRIS systems to include in your comparison, and how to test them before you decide.

Understanding HRIS Software in the Philippine Landscape

What an HRIS Is and How It Has Evolved

A Human Resource Information System, or HRIS, is a tool that automates and organizes HR tasks. In the past, most companies relied on spreadsheets and manual forms to manage employee data. Today’s HRIS platforms provide cloud storage, automated payroll, attendance tracking, employee self-service, recruitment, performance management, and many other features.

The exact scope varies by vendor and package. Payroll, attendance, recruitment and talent management may sit within the same platform, be sold as separate modules or depend on integrations. If you are unsure which category you need, see HRIS vs HRMS vs HCM in the Philippines.

The Realities of HR in the Philippines

Managing HR in the Philippines presents unique challenges. Local labor laws require accurate calculations of holiday pay, night differential, overtime, and rest day premiums. Organizations must also comply with DOLE, SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR.

Other common challenges include:

  • Multiple branch locations
  • Complex shift schedules
  • Large payroll cycles
  • Strict reporting requirements

An HRIS tailored for the Philippine market helps businesses remain compliant and consistent by automating calculations and generating necessary reports.

Why HRIS Adoption Matters for Filipino Companies

Efficiency and Productivity Gains

A modern HRIS minimizes repetitive tasks. Payroll processing, attendance checking, leave validation, and report creation can be done more quickly and with fewer mistakes. This frees up HR teams to focus on training programs, employee engagement, and workforce planning.

Cost Savings and Fewer Payroll Errors

Manual payroll can lead to expensive errors. Incorrect deductions or miscalculated hours often result in delays and disputes. Automated systems decrease errors and help companies avoid penalties for compliance issues.

Centralized and Secure Employee Data

Philippine businesses must comply with the Data Privacy Act, also known as RA 10173. An HRIS with secure access controls, encryption, and audit logs helps safeguard employee information. Centralized data management also enables HR teams to update records quickly.

Better Employee Engagement

Employee self-service features let staff file leave requests, view payslips, update personal information, and check attendance using their mobile devices. This boosts transparency and lowers the number of inquiries directed to HR.

How to Choose the Right HRIS in the Philippines

Step 1: Assess Your Company Size and Operational Needs

Different organizations have varying needs for HRIS solutions.

For SMEs

Small and medium companies often seek:

  • Affordable plans
  • Quick setup
  • Easy-to-use dashboards
  • Basic modules like timekeeping, payroll, and leave

For Large Enterprises

Larger organizations require:

  • API integrations
  • Custom approval workflows
  • Support for multiple locations and payroll groups
  • Advanced data management

Understanding your company size helps in identifying the appropriate type of HRIS.

Step 2: Check for Philippine Government Compliance

Compliance is one of the most critical HRIS features in the Philippines. A dependable system must support:

  • SSS contribution calculations
  • PhilHealth contribution tables
  • Pag-IBIG deductions and loan repayments
  • BIR withholding tax tables, Forms 2316 and 1601C
  • DOLE pay rules for holidays, overtime, and premiums

Systems that update automatically when new guidelines are released can help prevent errors and keep your payroll compliant.

Step 3: Evaluate Core HR and Payroll Features

A complete HRIS should offer strong capabilities in the following areas:

  • Employee database management
  • Automated payroll processing
  • Time and attendance integration
  • Leave and overtime workflows
  • Recruitment and onboarding tools
  • Performance management modules
  • Document and asset tracking
  • Reporting and analytics

These features help HR teams stay organized and deliver accurate results.

Step 4: Integration and Mobile Capabilities

Most companies use different tools for accounting, biometrics, and communication. Your HRIS should integrate easily with these systems.

Mobile access is also critical. Employees need an easy way to check payslips, submit requests, and log attendance.

Step 5: Scalability and Customization

A long-term HRIS should grow with your business. Look for features like:

  • Custom approval flows
  • Editable forms and fields
  • Multiple payroll groups
  • Support for various employment types

Flexible systems reduce the need for manual adjustments as operations expand.

Step 6: Data Security and Cloud Reliability

HR data must be secure at all times. A reliable HRIS should provide:

  • Encrypted data storage
  • Access control based on user roles
  • Regular backups
  • Disaster recovery systems
  • Compliance with RA 10173

Always inquire how your provider safeguards sensitive information.

Step 7: Strong Customer Support and Implementation

A good system is effective only when backed by knowledgeable support teams. Verify whether the provider offers:

  • Local customer service
  • Training programs
  • Guided implementation
  • Ongoing support for updates

Proper setup allows your organization to implement the system with confidence.

Other Providers to Consider

Smaller businesses may also look at GreatDay HR, MIHCM, Zoho People, Payruler, HReasily, Salarium and Omni HR. Some global systems offer advanced HR features but may not fully support Philippine payroll compliance, which can necessitate extra manual work.

Common Limitations Found Across Providers

Many HR platforms struggle with:

  • Detailed BIR and DOLE pay rules
  • Multiple payroll group requirements
  • Complex shift schedules
  • Localized report formats

The demo is where you find out whether a shortlisted system shares these limitations.

How to Run an HRIS Demo That Proves Fit

A typical HRIS demo lasts about 30 to 60 minutes and showcases payroll processing, attendance features, approval workflows, analytics, and the employee self-service portal. A vendor’s standard demo shows its strongest workflow. Give every shortlisted vendor the same scenarios instead, so you compare like with like.

Be sure to clarify these points during the demo:

  • Can workflows and approval routes be customized?
  • How quickly does the system update when SSS or BIR rules change?
  • What integration options are available?
  • How long does implementation typically take?
  • Where is data stored and how is it protected?

Compare the Handoffs That Create Work for HR

A system may perform well in individual modules while leaving gaps between them. An attendance record might be correct, for example, while the adjustment still requires a separate payroll upload.

Map one complete employee journey: onboarding, assignment to a location and roster, leave approval, attendance processing, payroll review and eventual exit. At each handoff, ask:

  • Does the information flow automatically, through an integration or through a manual file?
  • Who approves changes, and where is that approval recorded?
  • Can the next team see which information is final?
  • What happens when a correction arrives after the cut-off?

The value of connected HR software becomes clearer when you measure how much reconciliation remains between teams.

Test Payroll Using Difficult Cases

Payroll deserves a detailed evaluation because it brings together employee data, attendance, compensation and approvals. A clean payroll demonstration using standard salaries cannot establish fit for a complex workforce.

For Philippine operations, build test cases around the applicable requirements your payroll team already manages: statutory deductions and reporting, overtime, night differentials, holiday work, 13th month pay and year-end adjustments. Have your payroll specialist define the expected results using current rules and your approved policies. Our free Philippines tax calculator and salary calculator can help you prepare reference figures.

Then add operational exceptions: a mid-period joiner, a salary change, a late attendance correction and an employee moving between locations.

Ask the vendor to show the calculation inputs, review steps and correction process. If the result differs from your expected output, establish whether the cause is a configuration choice, an incorrect assumption or a system limitation.

The goal is a calculation your team can explain and verify. A compliance claim on a brochure is not enough evidence for your particular setup. For context on what a connected payroll module covers, see PeoplesHR payroll.

Check How Attendance Exceptions Reach Payroll

Attendance becomes harder to manage when employees work overnight, rotate across shifts or move between branches. The important test is how exceptions are resolved before they affect pay.

Consider an illustrative scenario: a 600-employee hospitality business operates across four properties. An employee swaps a night shift, misses a clock-out and submits a correction near payroll cut-off.

Ask every shortlisted vendor to demonstrate that same scenario. Follow the roster change, attendance exception, manager approval and payroll outcome. Record every manual intervention.

Evaluate Employee Access Alongside Administrative Control

Employee self-service can help reduce routine requests, but it must work for the people who will actually use it.

Have a frontline employee and a manager test common tasks: viewing a payslip, checking a leave balance, submitting a request and approving it. Use realistic devices and access conditions.

Then ask IT and HR to test permissions. Can a manager access only the appropriate employees? Can access change when someone transfers? Can sensitive salary information be restricted while ordinary team approvals remain available?

If AI features are part of the proposal, apply the same scrutiny. Test what information the feature can access, how permissions apply and whether an action needs approval before execution. Evaluate a specific task, rather than treating an AI label as evidence of usefulness.

Make Integrations and Reporting Part of the Demonstration

“Integration available” can describe very different arrangements. It might mean a standard connector, an API, a scheduled file exchange or a custom project.

For each connection to accounting, attendance devices or another business system, establish the method, timing, ownership and failure process. Ask what happens when an employee identifier does not match or a transfer fails.

Reporting needs the same practical treatment. Give vendors a sample report your business already uses. Ask them to recreate it and explain where its data comes from. Can HR and finance reconcile headcount, attendance and payroll information for the same period and organizational structure? If a report needs a separate spreadsheet each month, include that work in your evaluation.

Assess Implementation Before Choosing a Platform

A capable platform still needs a workable implementation plan. Confirm which employee records will be migrated, how historical information will be handled and who will validate it.

Your plan should identify configuration decisions, integration dependencies, user acceptance testing and payroll comparison runs. Agree on the conditions for go-live and the escalation route for unresolved issues.

Request a reference from a customer with comparable operating complexity. Ask about the work required from their internal team, the exceptions uncovered during testing and the support received after launch. A recognizable customer name alone does not establish relevance to your organization.

Use a Weighted Scorecard to Make the Decision

Start with mandatory requirements. A platform that fails a critical payroll, security or integration requirement should not win because its interface scores highly.

For systems that pass those requirements, the following is an illustrative weighting you can adapt:

Compare total cost separately over a consistent period. Include subscriptions, implementation, migration, integrations, training, support, additional modules and internal administration. Ask vendors to state assumptions and exclusions in writing.

FAQs About HRIS Software in the Philippines

What is the best HRIS in the Philippines?

There is no universal best HRIS. Build a shortlist around your payroll needs, workforce structure, approval processes, integration requirements and implementation capacity. Then compare systems using the same scenarios and evidence standards.

What is an HRIS and why do companies in the Philippines need it?

An HRIS is a system that automates HR functions such as payroll, attendance, employee records, and leave management. Philippine companies benefit from an HRIS because it reduces manual tasks, improves accuracy, and helps maintain compliance with SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, BIR, and DOLE regulations.

What features should an HRIS have for Philippine payroll compliance?

A suitable HRIS should support SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, BIR tax tables, holiday rules, night differential calculations, overtime rates, and accurate generation of government forms. Automatic updates when regulations change are also essential.

How do I know if an HRIS fits my company size and structure?

SMEs usually need a simple and cost-effective system with core HR modules. Larger companies require advanced workflows, multiple payroll groups, strong integrations, and scalability. The best approach is to map your processes and compare them with the HRIS features available.

Should payroll be included in an HRIS?

Connected payroll can simplify the movement of employee and attendance information. A separate payroll system may also be appropriate if the integration, ownership and reconciliation processes are clear. Evaluate the complete workflow and remaining manual work.

Are integrations with biometrics and accounting tools important?

Yes. Integrations help minimize duplicate data entry and enhance accuracy. Common integrations include biometric devices, accounting systems, ERP platforms, and mobile apps for attendance and self-service.

How many HRIS vendors should we compare?

A practical starting point is three or four vendors that meet your mandatory requirements. This leaves room for detailed testing and reference checks without spreading the evaluation team too thin.

Can a global HRIS support employees in the Philippines?

It may support Philippine employee records while using a different arrangement for local payroll. Confirm whether payroll is delivered directly, through a partner or through an integration, and document who owns updates, support and corrections.

How long does HRIS implementation usually take?

Implementation time varies by provider and company size. Smaller teams may be ready within weeks, while large enterprises may take longer due to training, configuration, and data migration. Clear onboarding support from the provider helps speed up the process.

What should we bring to an HRIS demo?

Bring anonymized workforce scenarios, approved policies, sample reports, integration requirements and expected test results. Avoid sharing identifiable employee data during early evaluation.
