13th Month Pay 2026: DOLE Labor Advisory No. 21 Explained

November 12, 2026 · Updated November 13, 2026 · By Nilendra Weerasinghe, CFA, Chief Strategy Officer, PeoplesHR · 10 min read

What DOLE Labor Advisory No. 21 (2026) requires

Private-sector employers in the Philippines must pay 13th month pay to every rank-and-file employee who worked at least one month in 2026, on or before 24 December 2026. The minimum is one-twelfth of the basic salary earned in the year. No exemption or deferment is allowed, and employers must report compliance on the DOLE portal by 15 January 2027.

Labor Secretary Francis N. Tolentino signed Labor Advisory No. 21, Series of 2026 on 6 November 2026. It restates the rules of Presidential Decree No. 851 and updates DOLE's worked examples to the new National Capital Region minimum wage of ₱755 a day under Wage Order No. NCR-28, which took effect on 26 September 2026.

One date needs planning: 24 December 2026 is a Thursday and an additional special non-working day under Proclamation No. 1006. The deadline itself does not move, so plan to release 13th month pay by Wednesday, 23 December.

What changed from 2025

The rules did not change. Coverage, the one-twelfth formula, the ban on exemption or deferment and the eight-item compliance report match the 2025 guidelines (Labor Advisory No. 16, Series of 2025). Four things are new:

Item20252026
Payment deadline24 December 202524 December 2026, now a special non-working day
Compliance report due15 January 202615 January 2027
Wage in DOLE's examples₱18,127.92 a month (₱695 a day on a six-day workweek)₱19,692.92 a month (₱755 a day on a six-day workweek, under Wage Order No. NCR-28 from 26 September 2026)
CommissionsNot addressedLeft out of the base for employees on a fixed wage plus commission, citing a Supreme Court case

Who is entitled to 13th month pay in 2026

Every rank-and-file employee in the private sector who worked at least one month in the calendar year is entitled. The advisory names these groups as covered:

  • Piece-rate workers
  • Employees paid a fixed or guaranteed wage plus commission
  • Employees with more than one employer
  • Employees who resigned or were terminated during the year
  • Employees on maternity leave who received the salary differential

How to compute 13th month pay

Divide the total basic salary earned during the year by 12.

13th month pay = total basic salary earned in the year ÷ 12

Basic salary includes all pay for services rendered. It excludes allowances and benefits not integrated into basic pay: the cash value of unused vacation and sick leave, overtime, premium pay, night shift differential, holiday pay and cost of living allowance. Those items count only if a contract, collective agreement, company practice or policy already treats them as part of basic salary.

DOLE's examples use the NCR minimum wage of ₱755 a day on a six-day workweek, which works out to ₱19,692.92 a month (₱755 × 313 days ÷ 12). Each day of unpaid leave lowers that month's basic salary by ₱755. Days of paid leave leave it unchanged.

Example A: regular employee with paid and unpaid leave

MonthAttendanceBasic salary earned (₱)

June5 days leave with pay19,692.92
July, AugustNo absence19,692.92 each

DecemberNo absence19,692.92
Total for the year228,010.04

₱228,010.04 ÷ 12 = ₱19,000.84 13th month pay.

Example B: piece-rate worker

The base is total earnings from output during the year. A piece-rate worker who earned ₱120,000.00 from output in 2026 gets ₱120,000.00 ÷ 12 = ₱10,000.00.

Example C: fixed wage plus commission

Only the fixed or guaranteed wage counts. The Supreme Court held in Boie-Takeda Chemicals, Inc. v. De la Serna (G.R. Nos. 92174 and 102552, 10 December 1993) that commissions are not part of basic salary for this purpose. An employee with ₱235,560.04 in fixed wages and ₱30,000.00 in commissions gets ₱235,560.04 ÷ 12 = ₱19,630.00. The fixed wage cannot be lower than the regional minimum wage.

Example D: employee who resigned in the year

A separated employee gets a pro-rated amount for the months worked. An employee who earned ₱177,236.28 from January to September and resigned at the end of September gets ₱177,236.28 ÷ 12 = ₱14,769.69.

In every case the result is a floor. Higher amounts under company policy, an employment contract or a collective bargaining agreement still apply.

Is 13th month pay taxable in 2026?

Up to ₱90,000 is tax-exempt, but the ceiling is shared. It covers 13th month pay and other benefits together under Section 32(B)(7)(e) of the Tax Code, as amended by the TRAIN Law. Bonuses, and any de minimis amounts above the 2026 ceilings set by Revenue Regulations No. 29-2025, count toward the same ₱90,000. The excess is taxable compensation.

House Bill 7661 proposes raising the ceiling to ₱150,000. It is a proposal, not law, so ₱90,000 applies to 2026 payouts. Check any employee's total with the 13th Month Calculator: ₱90,000 Check.

What goes in the DOLE compliance report

File the report on reports.dole.gov.ph by 15 January 2027. It must include:

  1. Name of establishment
  2. Address
  3. Principal product or business
  4. Total employment
  5. Total number of workers who received 13th month pay
  6. Amount granted per employee
  7. Total amount of benefits granted
  8. Name, position and telephone number of the person giving the information

Items 4 to 7 come straight from payroll, so pull them from the same run you paid from. The report and your payroll records should match line for line if DOLE inspects.

"24 December is a special non-working day this year, so release 13th month pay by 23 December. Before you do, recompute it for any December salary change or unpaid leave, so the amount you pay is the amount the employee earned."

Manolo Garcia-Ramos, Country Director – Philippines, PeoplesHR

Employer checklist for December 2026

  • Confirm who is rank-and-file and who worked at least one month in 2026, including leavers and new joiners
  • Build the basic salary base: earned basic pay only, after unpaid absences; no overtime, premiums, night differential, holiday pay or COLA unless policy integrates them
  • Use fixed wage only for commissioned staff, and total output earnings for piece-rate workers
  • Settle pro-rated 13th month for anyone who left in 2026
  • Check each employee against the ₱90,000 ceiling before the December annualization
  • Release payment by Wednesday, 23 December 2026, and keep proof of payment per employee
  • File the DOLE compliance report by 15 January 2027

The full October to March sequence, with every BIR and DOLE date, is in the Year-End Payroll Readiness Checklist: Philippines 2026.

4 common 13th month pay errors and how to prevent them

Computing the 13th month is rarely the hard part; the data behind it is. In PeoplesHR's year-end payroll webinar on 9 October 2026, a live poll of Philippine HR and payroll teams put annualization first, at 49%, ahead of government reporting, as their biggest year-end headache. 13th month pay is processed in the same December payroll as annualization, so a mistake in one can carry into the other.

In the same webinar, Sharon Aytona of Bridge PayDay Solutions named these as the mistakes she keeps seeing.

ErrorWhat goes wrongHow to prevent it
Incomplete year-to-date earningsThe base misses months or back pay, so the 13th month is understatedReconcile year-to-date basic pay per employee in November, before the December run
Late adjustmentsAn unpaid-leave or salary correction posted after the 13th month is computedClose adjustments for the year before computing, and recompute anyone changed after
Missing movementsNew joiners, leavers and salary changes left out, so leavers miss their pro-rated payPull every hire, separation and rate change for 2026 and check each against the payout list
Leaving it to DecemberEverything is left to December, when regular payroll, annualization and reporting all landValidate data in October, test in November, and leave December for final checks and payment

Frequently asked questions

When is the 13th month pay deadline in 2026?

On or before 24 December 2026, under DOLE Labor Advisory No. 21, Series of 2026. Because 24 December 2026 is a special non-working day, plan to pay by 23 December.

When is the DOLE 13th month compliance report due?

By 15 January 2027, filed online at reports.dole.gov.ph.

Can an employer apply for an exemption or defer payment?

No. The advisory states that no request for exemption or deferment will be accepted or allowed.

Do resigned or terminated employees get 13th month pay?

Yes. They receive a pro-rated amount based on the basic salary earned from January to their last day in 2026.

Do employees on maternity leave get 13th month pay?

Yes. Labor Advisory No. 21 lists employees who were on maternity leave and received salary differential among those entitled to 13th month pay.

Do lates, undertime and unpaid absences reduce 13th month pay?

Yes. 13th month pay is one-twelfth of the basic salary actually earned, so unpaid absences, lates and undertime that reduce basic salary also reduce it. In DOLE's own example, 10 days of leave without pay lower September's basic salary from ₱19,692.92 to ₱12,142.92.

Can 13th month pay be paid in parts?

Yes, as long as the full amount is paid on or before 24 December 2026. Some employers pay half mid-year and the rest in December. Put the schedule in a written company policy and keep to it.

What happens if 13th month pay is paid late?

The advisory allows no exemption or deferment. DOLE Regional, Field and Provincial Offices monitor compliance under Department Order No. 238, Series of 2023, and employees can raise unpaid or late 13th month pay with their DOLE office.

Are overtime, holiday pay and night differential included?

No, unless a contract, collective agreement, company practice or policy treats them as part of basic salary. The same applies to COLA and the cash value of unused leave.

Are commissions included in 13th month pay?

No. For employees paid a fixed wage plus commission, only the fixed or guaranteed wage counts, following Boie-Takeda Chemicals, Inc. v. De la Serna.

Is 13th month pay taxable in 2026?

The first ₱90,000 of 13th month pay and other benefits combined is tax-exempt. Amounts above that are taxable compensation.

Are managers entitled to 13th month pay?

The legal requirement covers rank-and-file employees. An employer may extend it to managers by policy or contract, and that policy then applies.

Mga madalas itanong (Tagalog)

Kailan ang deadline ng 13th month pay sa 2026?

Sa o bago ang 24 Disyembre 2026, ayon sa DOLE Labor Advisory No. 21, Series of 2026. Dahil special non-working day ang 24 Disyembre, mainam na ibigay ito hanggang 23 Disyembre.

Paano kinukuwenta ang 13th month pay?

Kabuuang basic salary na kinita sa taon, hatiin sa 12. Hindi kasama ang overtime, holiday pay, night differential at COLA, maliban kung itinuturing itong bahagi ng basic salary ng kontrata o patakaran ng kumpanya.

May 13th month pay ba ang nag-resign o natanggal sa trabaho?

Oo. Makakatanggap sila ng pro-rated na halaga batay sa basic salary na kinita mula Enero hanggang sa huling araw nila sa trabaho.

May buwis ba ang 13th month pay?

Walang buwis ang unang ₱90,000 ng pinagsamang 13th month pay at iba pang benepisyo. May buwis ang sobra rito.

Kailan ang deadline ng compliance report sa DOLE?

Hanggang 15 Enero 2027, sa reports.dole.gov.ph.
