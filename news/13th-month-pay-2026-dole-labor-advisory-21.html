13th Month Pay 2026: DOLE Labor Advisory No. 21 Explained

November 12, 2026 · Updated November 13, 2026 · By Nilendra Weerasinghe, CFA · 11 min read

What DOLE Labor Advisory No. 21 (2026) requires

Every private-sector employer in the Philippines must pay 13th month pay to its rank-and-file employees on or before 24 December 2026. That is the core of Labor Advisory No. 21, Series of 2026, which Labor Secretary Francis N. Tolentino signed on 6 November 2026. Any rank-and-file employee who worked at least one month during the year qualifies, the minimum due is one-twelfth of the basic salary they earned in 2026, and employers must then report their compliance through DOLE's online portal by 15 January 2027. DOLE has again made clear that it will not accept requests for exemption or deferment.

The advisory does not rewrite the law. It restates the rules of Presidential Decree No. 851 and refreshes DOLE's worked examples using the National Capital Region minimum wage of ₱755 a day, set by Wage Order No. NCR-28 and in force since 26 September 2026. For most payroll teams the substance will be familiar. What deserves attention this year is the calendar.

Under Proclamation No. 1006, 24 December 2026 is a Thursday and also an additional special non-working day. The legal deadline does not move, but releasing pay on a non-working day invites problems that are easy to avoid, so the sensible target is Wednesday, 23 December.

What changed from 2025

Anyone who ran 13th month pay last year will find little to relearn. Coverage, the one-twelfth formula, the prohibition on exemption or deferment and the eight items in the compliance report are all unchanged from the 2025 guidelines (Labor Advisory No. 16, Series of 2025). The differences lie in the dates, in the wage DOLE uses for its examples and in one clarification on commissions.

Item20252026
Payment deadline24 December 202524 December 2026, now a special non-working day

CommissionsNot addressedLeft out of the base for employees on a fixed wage plus commission, citing a Supreme Court case

Who is entitled to 13th month pay in 2026

Entitlement turns on two questions: is the employee rank-and-file, and did they work for at least one month during the calendar year? If the answer to both is yes, they are owed 13th month pay regardless of their position, employment status or the way their pay is calculated. The advisory goes further and names several groups that employers sometimes overlook:

  • Piece-rate workers
  • Employees paid a fixed or guaranteed wage plus commission
  • Employees with more than one employer
  • Employees who resigned or were terminated during the year
  • Employees on maternity leave who received the salary differential

Managers sit outside the legal requirement. Many employers extend the benefit to them anyway through policy or contract, and where they do, that commitment must be honoured.

How to compute 13th month pay

The formula itself is simple: add up the basic salary an employee actually earned between January and December, then divide by 12.

13th month pay = total basic salary earned in the year ÷ 12

The judgement lies in deciding what counts as basic salary. It covers remuneration for services rendered, but not allowances and monetary benefits that are not integrated into basic pay. In practice, overtime, premium pay, night shift differential, holiday pay, cost of living allowance and the cash value of unused vacation and sick leave all stay out of the base, unless an employment contract, collective agreement, company practice or policy already treats them as part of basic salary.

Attendance matters as well, because the formula looks at salary earned rather than salary contracted. DOLE's examples use the NCR minimum wage of ₱755 a day on a six-day workweek, which comes to ₱19,692.92 a month (₱755 × 313 days ÷ 12). Every day of unpaid leave reduces that month's basic salary by ₱755, while paid leave counts as salary earned and leaves the month whole. The four examples below, all taken from the advisory, show how this works in practice.

Example A: regular employee with paid and unpaid leave

Consider an employee who took five days of paid leave in June, ten days of unpaid leave in September and one unpaid day in November.

MonthAttendanceBasic salary earned (₱)
January to MayNo absence19,692.92 each
June5 days leave with pay19,692.92
July, AugustNo absence19,692.92 each
September10 days leave without pay12,142.92
OctoberNo absence19,692.92
November1 day leave without pay18,937.92
DecemberNo absence19,692.92
Total for the year228,010.04

Only the unpaid days reduce the base. September falls to ₱12,142.92 and November to ₱18,937.92, while June, despite five days away, stays whole. The year's basic salary comes to ₱228,010.04, and ₱228,010.04 ÷ 12 gives a 13th month pay of ₱19,000.84.

Example B: piece-rate worker

For employees paid by output, the base is their total earnings from output over the year. A piece-rate worker who earned ₱120,000.00 in 2026 is therefore owed ₱120,000.00 ÷ 12 = ₱10,000.00.

Example C: fixed wage plus commission

Where an employee earns a fixed or guaranteed wage plus commission, only the fixed wage enters the calculation. The 2026 advisory grounds this in the Supreme Court's decision in Boie-Takeda Chemicals, Inc. v. De la Serna (G.R. Nos. 92174 and 102552, 10 December 1993), which held that commissions are not part of basic salary for this purpose. An employee who earned ₱235,560.04 in fixed wages and ₱30,000.00 in commissions is owed ₱235,560.04 ÷ 12 = ₱19,630.00. The fixed wage itself may not fall below the applicable regional minimum wage.

Example D: employee who resigned in the year

Employees who leave before December are still owed a pro-rated amount for the time they worked. Someone who earned ₱177,236.28 between January and September and resigned at the end of September is owed ₱177,236.28 ÷ 12 = ₱14,769.69.

Each of these results is a minimum. Where company policy, an employment contract or a collective bargaining agreement promises more, the higher amount applies.

Is 13th month pay taxable in 2026?

The tax treatment needs more care than the computation, because the exemption is not reserved for 13th month pay alone. Under Section 32(B)(7)(e) of the Tax Code, as amended by the TRAIN Law, up to ₱90,000 of 13th month pay and other benefits combined is tax-exempt. Bonuses count against the same ceiling, as do any de minimis benefits paid above the 2026 thresholds set by Revenue Regulations No. 29-2025. Whatever exceeds ₱90,000 is taxable compensation and has to be reflected in the December annualization.

That makes the order of work important. Know each employee's running total of exempt benefits before you finalise the 13th month run, not after it. Our 13th Month Calculator: ₱90,000 Check shows where any individual employee stands.

House Bill 7661 would raise the ceiling to ₱150,000, but it remains a proposal. Until it becomes law, the ₱90,000 ceiling applies to 2026 payouts.

What goes in the DOLE compliance report

Once employees are paid, the obligation turns to reporting. Employers must file a compliance report on the DOLE Online Compliance Portal, reports.dole.gov.ph, by 15 January 2027, covering eight items:

  1. Name of establishment
  2. Address
  3. Principal product or business
  4. Total employment
  5. Total number of workers who received 13th month pay
  6. Amount granted per employee
  7. Total amount of benefits granted
  8. Name, position and telephone number of the person giving the information

Items 4 to 7 come directly from payroll. Take them from the same run you paid from rather than rebuilding them separately, so that the report and your payroll records agree line for line if DOLE ever compares the two.

"24 December is a special non-working day this year, so release 13th month pay by 23 December. Before you do, recompute it for any December salary change or unpaid leave, so the amount you pay is the amount the employee earned."

Manolo Garcia-Ramos, Country Director – Philippines, PeoplesHR

Employer checklist for December 2026

With the rules settled, the December work comes down to seven steps.

  • Confirm who is rank-and-file and who worked at least one month in 2026, including leavers and new joiners
  • Build the basic salary base from earned basic pay only, after unpaid absences, leaving out overtime, premiums, night differential, holiday pay and COLA unless policy integrates them
  • Use the fixed wage only for commissioned staff, and total output earnings for piece-rate workers
  • Settle pro-rated 13th month pay for anyone who left in 2026
  • Check each employee against the ₱90,000 ceiling before the December annualization
  • Release payment by Wednesday, 23 December 2026, and keep proof of payment for each employee
  • File the DOLE compliance report by 15 January 2027

4 common 13th month pay errors and how to prevent them

The arithmetic is rarely what goes wrong. The data feeding it is. When we polled Philippine HR and payroll teams during PeoplesHR's year-end payroll webinar on 9 October 2026, 49% named annualization as their biggest year-end headache, ahead of government reporting. 13th month pay runs through the same December payroll, so an error in one can easily carry into the other.

Sharon Aytona of Bridge PayDay Solutions, who joined us for that session, identified four mistakes she sees year after year.

ErrorWhat goes wrongHow to prevent it
Incomplete year-to-date earningsThe base misses months or back pay, so the 13th month is understatedReconcile year-to-date basic pay for each employee in November, before the December run
Late adjustmentsAn unpaid-leave or salary correction is posted after the 13th month has been computedClose the year's adjustments before computing, and recompute anyone changed afterwards
Missing movementsNew joiners, leavers and salary changes are left out, so leavers miss their pro-rated payPull every hire, separation and rate change for 2026 and check each one against the payout list
Leaving it to DecemberEverything waits for December, when regular payroll, annualization and reporting all fall due togetherValidate data in October, test in November, and keep December for final checks and payment

Frequently asked questions

When is the 13th month pay deadline in 2026?

On or before 24 December 2026, under DOLE Labor Advisory No. 21, Series of 2026. Because 24 December is a special non-working day this year, plan to pay by 23 December.

When is the DOLE 13th month compliance report due?

By 15 January 2027, filed online through the DOLE Online Compliance Portal at reports.dole.gov.ph.

Can an employer apply for an exemption or defer payment?

No. The advisory states that no request for exemption or deferment will be accepted or allowed.

Do resigned or terminated employees get 13th month pay?

Yes. They receive a pro-rated amount based on the basic salary they earned from January to their last day of work in 2026.

Do employees on maternity leave get 13th month pay?

Yes. Labor Advisory No. 21 expressly lists employees on maternity leave who received the salary differential among those entitled to 13th month pay.

Do lates, undertime and unpaid absences reduce 13th month pay?

Yes. 13th month pay is one-twelfth of the basic salary actually earned, so unpaid absences, lates and undertime that reduce basic salary reduce it too. In DOLE's own example, 10 days of leave without pay lower September's basic salary from ₱19,692.92 to ₱12,142.92.

Can 13th month pay be paid in parts?

Yes, provided the full amount is paid on or before 24 December 2026. Some employers pay half mid-year and the balance in December. If you do, set out the schedule in a written company policy and apply it consistently.

What happens if 13th month pay is paid late?

The advisory allows no exemption or deferment. DOLE Regional, Field and Provincial Offices monitor compliance under Department Order No. 238, Series of 2023, and employees can raise unpaid or late 13th month pay with their DOLE office.

Are overtime, holiday pay and night differential included?

No, unless a contract, collective agreement, company practice or policy treats them as part of basic salary. The same applies to COLA and the cash value of unused leave.

Are commissions included in 13th month pay?

No. For employees paid a fixed wage plus commission, only the fixed or guaranteed wage counts, following Boie-Takeda Chemicals, Inc. v. De la Serna.

Is 13th month pay taxable in 2026?

Up to ₱90,000 of 13th month pay and other benefits combined is tax-exempt. Anything above that is taxable compensation.

Are managers entitled to 13th month pay?

Not by law. The requirement covers rank-and-file employees, but an employer may extend 13th month pay to managers by policy or contract, and that commitment must then be honoured.

Mga madalas itanong (Tagalog)

Kailan ang deadline ng 13th month pay sa 2026?

Sa o bago ang 24 Disyembre 2026, ayon sa DOLE Labor Advisory No. 21, Series of 2026. Dahil special non-working day ang 24 Disyembre, mainam na ibigay ito hanggang 23 Disyembre.

Paano kinukuwenta ang 13th month pay?

Kabuuang basic salary na kinita sa taon, hatiin sa 12. Hindi kasama ang overtime, holiday pay, night differential at COLA, maliban kung itinuturing itong bahagi ng basic salary ng kontrata o patakaran ng kumpanya.

May 13th month pay ba ang nag-resign o natanggal sa trabaho?

Oo. Makakatanggap sila ng pro-rated na halaga batay sa basic salary na kinita mula Enero hanggang sa huling araw nila sa trabaho.

May buwis ba ang 13th month pay?

Walang buwis ang unang ₱90,000 ng pinagsamang 13th month pay at iba pang benepisyo. May buwis ang sobra rito.

Kailan ang deadline ng compliance report sa DOLE?

Hanggang 15 Enero 2027, sa reports.dole.gov.ph.
